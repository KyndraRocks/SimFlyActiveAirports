SimFly Telemetry Bridge

A small free companion app that puts your Microsoft Flight Simulator aircraft on the SimFly Active Airports map — including readings the browser cannot otherwise reach.

What it does

  • Shows your live aircraft on the map, updating once a second.
  • Adds height above ground and indicated altitude — neither of which FSUIPC can supply correctly.
  • Adds true airspeed, Mach, vertical speed, barometric pressure, outside air temperature, wind, fuel, and your actual aircraft name.
  • Lets altitude alerts trigger on height above ground, which is what you want for terrain- and pattern-relative cues.

Installing it

  • Download and run it. There is nothing to configure and no account to create.
  • It installs for you alone — no administrator prompt.
  • It needs no firewall rule, and Windows will not ask for one.
  • It bundles everything it needs — no .NET download.
  • It starts with Windows and runs invisibly. There is no window and no tray icon.
  • Then open Active Airports, and click 🛰 SimConnect Live in the 🔌 Live menu — or just load a flight plan and let it connect itself.

Requires 64-bit Windows 10 or 11 and Microsoft Flight Simulator (2020 or 2024).

Windows will warn you — this is expected

The installer is not code-signed, because a certificate costs several hundred dollars a year for a free hobby app. Windows therefore shows “Windows protected your PC” and calls the publisher unknown. To continue, click More info and then Run anyway.

If you would rather verify the file first, the SHA-256 checksum of the exact file served above is published in the box at the top of this page. In PowerShell:
Get-FileHash .\SimFlyTelemetryBridge-Setup-*.exe -Algorithm SHA256

What it does not do

  • It never writes to the simulator. The connection is strictly read-only, so it cannot affect your flight.
  • It never sends anything over the internet. Its server accepts connections only from your own computer, and it checks for updates never — Active Airports does that on its behalf.
  • It collects no analytics of any kind.
  • It stays out of the way of motion-platform software: it asks the simulator for data once a second at below-normal priority, and does not connect to the simulator at all until you actually open the map.

If something goes wrong

  • Visit http://127.0.0.1:8975/ for a plain “it’s running” page.
  • That page has a Copy diagnostic report button — paste its output into an issue. It contains no flight data.
  • The Start Menu also has Diagnostics (runs it in a console window so you can watch it work) and Save Diagnostic Report (writes a zip to your desktop).

Removing it

Uninstall from Add/Remove Programs. That removes the program, its scheduled startup task, and its settings folder.
